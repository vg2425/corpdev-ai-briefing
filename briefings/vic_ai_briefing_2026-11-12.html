CoreWeave beats and raises on Q3, swears off unsecured debt through 2027, flags a high investment grade DDTL at a 7 to 7.5 percent yield and about 9 billion dollars of prepayments, and the stock closes up about 25 percent

CoreWeave reported Q3 after the close on November 10. Per the earnings call transcript and TradingKey's read of the release: revenue of 3.661 billion dollars, up 168 percent year over year and above the high end of guidance; adjusted operating income of 379 million dollars against 200 to 260 million dollars guided, a 10 percent margin (1 percent in Q1, 5 percent in Q2); adjusted EBITDA of 2.3 billion dollars at a 62 percent margin; GAAP net loss of 709 million dollars on 846 million dollars of interest expense; Q3 capex of 14.5 billion dollars; backlog of about 132.5 billion dollars. FY2026 revenue guidance moves to 13.0 to 13.3 billion dollars, adjusted operating income to 1.275 to 1.375 billion dollars, capex to 37 to 39 billion dollars, with an exit ARR of 20 to 21 billion dollars. On financing, management said it will not issue parent level unsecured debt at current yields through 2027, expects to launch a new DDTL "in the coming weeks" with multiple agencies indicating a high investment grade rating at an implied 7 to 7.5 percent yield financing more than 90 percent of associated capex, and expects about 9 billion dollars of prepayments and deposits in the next three months, more than 3 billion dollars of it on fully paid off recontracted fleet. Shares closed November 11 at 60.77 dollars, up 24.71 percent, per TradingKey. Flag: early after hours coverage reported gains of 1.8 to 5.7 percent before the regular session rerated the stock, and adjusted EPS is reported as minus 1.27 dollars in one source and minus 0.95 dollars in another.

Read analysis ▾

This print answered the two questions Monday's edition said mattered most: whether the delivery machine is working and whether the company can still fund itself after the credit pullback. On delivery, active power is above 1.7 gigawatts (doubled since year end 2025), more than 850 megawatts came online year to date, construction in progress fell from 11.9 to 9.3 billion dollars, and management said it sees no material risk to 2027 deliveries at fully permitted sites. On funding, the pitch is a two track stack: asset level DDTLs matched to customer contracts (225 to 550 basis points over SOFR since IPO, with investment grade and enterprise customers backing more than 75 percent of committed capex through 2027) plus prepayments (collected on 70 percent of deals this year). The weighted average cost of debt is 7.9 percent, down 250 basis points year over year, and the company expects to raise less than 5 billion dollars of incremental parent level capital through 2027.

The pricing disclosures are the most useful numbers for anyone benchmarking compute economics. Management said SKU pricing rose about 25 percent in July and another 10 percent later in the quarter, Ampere and Hopper pricing rose 30 to 50 percent quarter over quarter, short term capacity is selling at more than 40 million dollars per megawatt per year, and new four and five year contracts are moving to 20 to 25 million dollars per megawatt. About 130,000 GPUs roll off contract in 2027 and about 170,000 in 2028; at current pricing, management sized the renewal opportunity at approaching 4 billion dollars of incremental annualized revenue for 2027 and nearly 10 billion dollars including 2028, outside the reported backlog. The company also said demand is ten times available capacity through 2027 and that it has not yet brought 2028 capacity to market.

Skeptic's note: the confirmed figures are the reported results and guidance. The DDTL rating, its 7 to 7.5 percent yield, the 9 billion dollars of prepayments, the renewal revenue sizing and the "10 times" demand claim are management expectations, not executed transactions. Short term pricing at 40 million dollars per megawatt is a scarcity price that flatters margins while supply is tight and is exactly what reverses if the "wall of supply" Goldman's analyst asked about arrives. Q4 interest expense is guided to 1.05 to 1.15 billion dollars, so adjusted net loss persists until the first half of 2028 by the company's own timeline. Notably, Microsoft was not mentioned anywhere in the transcript reviewed here; no analyst asked about the Bloomberg and CNBC reports on neocloud consolidation, and management volunteered nothing about Microsoft's share of backlog. Flag: the FT's November 11 report on the chip backed loan, relayed by TradingKey, describes it as funding hardware for an unnamed investment grade client; the FT article itself was not read.

Why It Matters for CoreWeave

Bull read: the quarter reframes the debate from "can neoclouds survive the credit window" to "the scaled incumbent is pricing up and self funding." Swearing off unsecured paper while the IG DDTL market stays open removes the 15 percent plus yield overhang from the equity story, and the recontracting math on paid off fleet is a cash engine no smaller rival has yet. Threat read: margin expansion is leaning on short term and premium pricing in a scarcity window; floating rate DDTLs reprice if the Fed hikes in December (see story 07); and silence on Microsoft leaves the customer concentration question open for the next turn of the news cycle. For corporate development, the near term to do list is the DDTL launch (rating, spread, anchor customer identity) and a prepared answer on Microsoft's share of the 132.5 billion dollar backlog.

Nvidia fell about 8 percent after hours (CNN had it down 7.2 percent at 7 p.m. ET after a drop of more than 9 percent; other tallies say 8.27 and 8.61 percent), erasing a 2.87 percent regular session gain. Separately, President Trump said Monday the FBI is looking into the Alice Huang matter, Baird downgraded the stock to Neutral with a 200 dollar target (from 500), and Huang faces a requested November 18 House briefing. Flag: Nvidia's Q3 report is dated November 17 by most sources and November 16 by one.

Read analysis ▾

Under Nvidia's bylaws the board can remove the CEO with or without cause by majority vote, the board has eleven members of whom ten are independent, Huang owns about 3.6 percent with no super voting shares, and the company has no board chair. That governance structure is why the market moved from treating this as a scandal to pricing it as a leadership change. A Minotaur Capital cofounder told Reuters the market is starting to price Nvidia "without its founder as the likely outcome, not a tail risk." Al Jazeera puts the week's market value loss above 600 billion dollars, and Nvidia was down 27 percent from its early October peak at Wednesday's close.

The China thread matters as much as the personal one. Megaspeed bought about 4.8 billion dollars of Nvidia equipment per the NYT, U.S. authorities have investigated it since 2024 over suspected export control evasion (Megaspeed denies this), and Baird's downgrade flagged possible new U.S. restrictions on H200 exports. Huang's attendance at the APEC summit in Shenzhen on November 18 and 19 is now in doubt per the South China Morning Post. Any CEO change would land the same week as earnings and the requested congressional briefing.

Skeptic's note: confirmed facts are Huang's own admission of infidelity (November 8 memo), the board's independent investigation and its November 8 statement of confidence, and the share price moves. The WSJ ouster report, the Bloomberg successor search, the second relationship and the Kress and Buck shortlist are all sourced to unnamed people. A board meeting is not a decision. Nothing reported so far alleges that Nvidia's technical or customer information was compromised, and the board said on November 8 it had no reason to believe it was. Flag: the 8 percent after hours figure varies by timestamp across sources.

Why It Matters for CoreWeave

Threat read: Nvidia is CoreWeave's critical supplier, a strategic relationship, and the reason CoreWeave was first to put Vera Rubin into production (with Cognition, per Tuesday's call). Huang personally championed allocation to neoclouds; a successor, particularly one installed under congressional and China export scrutiny, could revisit allocation policy, pricing, or the 49 percent China cap guidance FT reported on November 3. An Nvidia drawdown also drags the whole AI infrastructure complex and GPU collateral values that underpin DDTLs. Bull read: operational continuity at Nvidia does not depend on one person in the next two quarters, Rubin supply is already contracted, and a governance reset could reduce the export control overhang that has spooked lenders. Watch for any interim CEO announcement before or alongside the November 17 print and whether guidance language on cloud partner allocation changes.

Two weeks ago the debate was whether a selloff and a hostile Congress would push this deal out. Instead the range came in well below the 2 trillion dollars floated in September but the book appears strong, and the deal helped lift the whole AI complex on November 11 alongside CoreWeave's print. The strategic tranche is the structurally interesting part: every major supplier in Anthropic's compute chain (chips, memory, networking, foundry, and two of its cloud hosts) is buying equity at the IPO price. That is vendor financing by another name and it ties suppliers' fortunes more tightly to one customer's success.

The filing also carries an unusual safety disclosure. Fortune reports Anthropic said internal estimates indicate it "may imminently meet" its Responsible Scaling Policy definition of dramatically accelerated progress attributable to AI, roughly a doubling of the pace of progress, that no current model has crossed it, and that it is considering policy changes to keep humans in control. That is a risk factor investors will price and regulators in the incoming Democratic Congress will read.

Skeptic's note: confirmed facts are the price range, share count, the strategic placement amounts as stated in the filing, TSMC's 500 million dollar board approval, and the run rate disclosure. Oversubscription, the pricing date and the 90.4 billion dollar combined size come from people familiar. Run rate revenue is an annualized snapshot, not GAAP revenue, and Fortune's November 2 coverage said Q3 growth slowed and costs jumped. Flag: one KuCoin item claims the roadshow deck mentions compute 49 times and a 35 percent long term margin target; single source, not used.

Why It Matters for CoreWeave

Bull read: up to 64 billion dollars of primary capital plus 26 billion dollars of strategic money lands with a lab whose growth is compute constrained, and much of it will be spent on capacity in 2027 and 2028, exactly the window CoreWeave says it has not yet brought to market. A successful print also resets the valuation anchor for AI infrastructure equity broadly. Threat read: Anthropic is building its own power and site portfolio (the 1.7 gigawatt generator orders and the Nscale Monarch capacity covered Monday), and a lab flush with public equity has more option to self build or to bargain hard. Microsoft and Nvidia appearing on the strategic list also deepens the hyperscaler and supplier lock in that neoclouds compete against. A frontier price war in 48 hours: Anthropic ships Claude Fable 5.2 and Mythos 5.2 at a 20 percent price cut, and Google makes Gemini 4 Carbon generally available at one quarter of Fable's rates with a multi agent "/swarm" preview

On November 9 Anthropic released Claude Fable 5.2 (all platforms) and Mythos 5.2 (vetted organizations via its Life Sciences and Cyber Verification Programs), citing better coding, reasoning, vision and agent team orchestration, outputs up to 30 percent faster, and pricing of 8 dollars per million input tokens and 40 dollars per million output (5.1 was 10 and 50 dollars, per VentureBeat's table). Per VentureBeat, Fable 5.2 now leads the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index at 65 versus Carbon at 60. Also this week: Alibaba previewed Qwen4 Max, MiniMax released open H3 Image weights, and Anthropic launched Claude Sheets, Whiteboards, Data and a Claude Meetings beta. Flag: some coverage dates Carbon's "launch" to October 28; November 10 is the general availability date.

Read analysis ▾

The pricing story matters more than the leaderboard. VentureBeat's per task analysis shows why token price is a poor proxy for cost: on Artificial Analysis's suite Carbon at high reasoning averages about 7.76 dollars per task versus 26.54 dollars for Fable at maximum effort, but Fable at medium effort is cheaper than Carbon at medium. On Zapier's AutomationBench Carbon leads at 53.88 percent and about 2.12 dollars per task versus Fable 5.2 at 51.45 percent and 4.64 dollars. Both launches push the same architecture: an expensive frontier model as orchestrator or auditor directing cheaper models doing parallel implementation. Anthropic explicitly positions Fable as an advisor and orchestrator with Opus 5.5 as the daily driver; Google recommends Argon for most work with Carbon on coordination. Agent swarms multiply calls per task, which is the mechanism by which falling per token prices still raise total inference compute.

⚡ Medium Signal Power & Policy Markets 9 sources
Washington's mixed signals: the Senate advances the permitting bill 73 to 25 with "data centers pay their own way" provisions, but analysts now give it about a 25 percent chance, while a hot October CPI leaves a December Fed hike almost fully priced

On November 9 the Senate invoked cloture 73 to 25 on the motion to proceed to H.R. 2347, the vehicle for the Bipartisan American Affordability and Jobs Act. Per Utility Dive and CNBC, the bill would curb litigation against energy projects, limit states' and EPA's ability to preemptively block them, expand federal transmission siting, and require data center builders to pay for the grid infrastructure they use. TD Cowen cut its odds of success from about 40 to about 25 percent after the midterms; Jefferies wrote that "Senate passage is possible, but enactment remains unlikely without a clear House strategy." On rates, October CPI printed on November 10 with core at 0.3 percent month over month, the level at which Governor Waller had said a 50 basis point December hike should be discussed; CME FedWatch per TradingKey shows a December hike almost fully priced with about a 15 percent chance of 50 basis points. Separately, Microsoft's Noelle Walsh said Microsoft will bear the power costs of its data center expansion itself. Flag: TradingKey ties that pledge to Project Kilby in West Texas, but the Chevron Kilby agreement itself dates to June 22, 2026 and is not new.

Read analysis ▾

The permitting bill is the lame duck Congress's only real vehicle for speeding energy and transmission buildout before Democrats take both chambers in January. Jefferies' read of the manager's amendment is specific and relevant: incrementally better for transmission, nuclear and offshore wind, and worse for "large BTM gas configurations," the behind the meter gas model several AI campuses (and Anthropic's reported generator orders) rely on. Senator Merkley explicitly criticized the bill for making it easier to build data centers on behind the meter gas. Reuters separately reported investors in AI related debt are growing more cautious as Congress prepares to scrutinize data centers.

The rate path matters directly. CoreWeave's DDTLs price as spreads over SOFR, so a December hike, and Lorie Logan's call for two more 25 basis point hikes, raises the coupon on floating facilities and the yield on the next DDTL management guided at 7 to 7.5 percent "based on current SOFR." Chicago Fed President Goolsbee called the inflation report "unsettling."

Skeptic's note: confirmed facts are the cloture vote count, the bill's text as described by multiple outlets, and the CPI print. Odds of passage are analyst estimates. FedWatch probabilities move daily. The Microsoft power pledge is a public statement, not a binding tariff commitment. Flag: the EIA electricity forecast cited in The Neuron (4,216 billion kWh in 2025 rising to 4,370 billion kWh in 2027) differs from October STEO coverage (4,195 rising to 4,399 billion kWh); verify against the November STEO directly.

Why It Matters for CoreWeave

Threat read: if the bill dies, the 2027 policy baseline is a Democratic Congress expected to introduce bills targeting data center development, plus Texas under Governor elect Hinojosa, while rate hikes lift CoreWeave's floating cost of debt just as it leans harder on secured facilities. Bull read: CoreWeave told analysts the overwhelming majority of its Texas power is online or fully permitted and under construction, with more than 1.3 gigawatts of contracted power outside the U.S.; tighter U.S. permitting raises the scarcity value of energized capacity and paid off fleet. "Pay your own way" tariffs favour operators who contract power directly over those relying on cheap grid interconnection. Ask: what share of CoreWeave's DDTL principal is floating versus hedged.

⚡ Medium Signal Agentic AI & Tooling AI Infrastructure & Chips 7 sources
Fireworks AI widens its breach disclosure: attackers came in through an unnamed third party cloud provider hosting its R&D machines and reached customer secrets, datasets and partial model weights

Fireworks expanded its disclosure on November 9. Per its updated bulletin as relayed by RuntimeWire and others: unauthorized use of internal credentials was identified October 6; separately, a third party cloud provider hosting Fireworks R&D machines had a security incident on October 23, which the attacker used on October 24 to reach Fireworks development machines and run an unauthorized workload. Account and billing data were accessed, and for a limited set of customers, secrets, datasets and incomplete model weights. Fireworks says Inference, the Training SDK and Nexus (zero data retention by default) show no detected impact. Unit 42 is investigating and law enforcement was notified. The provider has not been named and the number of affected customers has not been disclosed. Context: Railway disclosed on October 21 that attackers decrypted a subset of customer secrets in a campaign it believes targeted AI provider API keys. Flag: The Neuron cites about 740,000 dollars of fraudulent AI charges at Railway; that figure could not be confirmed elsewhere and is not used.

Read analysis ▾

"LLMjacking," stealing AI keys and compute access to resell tokens or run distillation, is becoming its own criminal market, and inference platforms that sit on top of rented GPU clouds have layered supply chains. The Fireworks timeline is the first major case this year where the initial hop was an upstream cloud provider rather than the platform itself. Wiz's "Black October" roundup counts nine AI platform incidents in a month.

Skeptic's note: confirmed facts are Fireworks' own bulletin claims as summarized by secondary outlets (the primary bulletin could not be fetched in full here). The identity of the cloud provider is unknown, and nothing reported links it to CoreWeave or any specific neocloud. Treat any speculation naming a provider as unverified.

Why It Matters for CoreWeave

Threat read: until the provider is named, every GPU cloud that hosts inference platforms will get the diligence question from enterprise buyers, and an incoming Congress focused on AI oversight will treat compute providers as part of the security perimeter. Bull read: CoreWeave is selling Forge and managed inference to the Fortune 500 (10 percent are now customers, per the call); demonstrable tenant isolation and a clean incident record are a differentiator against smaller clouds and a reason enterprises consolidate on scaled providers. Worth preparing a short customer facing note on isolation controls before procurement teams ask.
