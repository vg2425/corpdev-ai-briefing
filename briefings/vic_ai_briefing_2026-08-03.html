Amazon settles last week's capex tiebreaker in Microsoft's favor: AWS reaccelerates to 37 percent, capex guides up to about 220 billion dollars, and the stock has its best day since 2012

Amazon reported second quarter results after the close on July 30 and the print resolved the Microsoft versus Meta debate that opened Thursday's edition. Total revenue rose about 20 percent to roughly 200.6 billion dollars, operating income came in near 27.5 billion dollars, and AWS grew about 37 percent to roughly 42.2 billion dollars, described as its fastest growth in 18 quarters and ahead of forecasts. Amazon raised its 2026 cash capex expectation to about 220 billion dollars from a prior figure near 200 billion dollars, and reported an AWS order backlog cited around 496 billion dollars. Shares jumped sharply on July 31, with coverage citing moves in the 12 to 15 percent range and Bloomberg calling it the biggest one day gain since 2012. One important asterisk: reported net income near 62.6 billion dollars was flattered by a non operating gain of roughly 53.4 billion dollars tied largely to Amazon's Anthropic stake, and trailing 12 month free cash flow swung to an outflow reported near 7.6 billion dollars.

Read analysis ▾

Thursday's edition framed Amazon as the tiebreaker between two readings of the capex regime: Microsoft's discipline rewarded, and Meta's rising floor punished. Amazon came down firmly on the Microsoft side. The market did not reward Amazon for spending less, because it is spending more, guiding capex up toward 220 billion dollars. It rewarded Amazon because the cloud line accelerated and the company disclosed a very large backlog, which together let investors argue the spend is demand led rather than speculative. Accelerating cloud plus a visible backlog was enough to turn a capex increase into a positive, which is the exact opposite of how Meta's raise was received a day earlier.

The asterisk deserves as much weight as the headline. A large share of the reported profit came from a paper gain on the Anthropic investment, not from operations, and trailing free cash flow was negative. So the cleanest read is narrower than the stock move suggests: AWS demand genuinely reaccelerated and the backlog is real signal, but the bottom line quality this quarter was helped by a mark to market gain on a private AI stake. Both things are true at once.

Skeptic's note: revenue, AWS growth, operating income and the capex guide are reported results and are relatively firm, though the exact backlog and capex figures vary slightly across outlets. The roughly 53.4 billion dollar Anthropic gain and the negative free cash flow figure are as reported and should be confirmed against the official release and filing. The 12 to 15 percent stock move is an intraday to single session snapshot that can settle differently. Treat the direction, AWS reacceleration validating the capex, as the confirmed signal and the precise numbers as provisional.

Why It Matters for CoreWeave

This is the most important macro read of the week for CoreWeave and it is mostly bullish. AWS reaccelerating to 37 percent with a roughly half trillion dollar backlog is direct evidence that demand for exactly the compute CoreWeave rents is not slowing, and the market rewarding a capex increase because cloud growth justified it is the friendly regime for a debt funded builder. The neocloud complex snapped back hard on the same catalyst, covered in story two. The threat sits in the asterisk and in the structure. Part of the celebrated return was a paper gain on an AI stake rather than cash, free cash flow was negative, and the same hyperscalers posting these numbers are increasingly financing their own fleets. If the market later discounts investment gains and refocuses on cash generation, the cost of capital pressure that repriced CoreWeave's Anthropic linked loan last week returns quickly.

The neocloud complex had a violent two day round trip. On July 30 the same names snapped back hard, with 24/7 Wall St reporting Nebius up about 27 percent, CoreWeave up about 24 percent and Oracle up about 7 percent as AI cloud names rebounded. The rebound coincided with two catalysts: CoreWeave's Leidos federal partnership, covered in story three, and building anticipation into Amazon's after the close print that ultimately showed AWS reaccelerating. In other words, a sentiment driven sell off on financing fear reversed on a demand signal within 24 hours.

Read analysis ▾

This is the clearest illustration yet of what CoreWeave's stock has become: a high beta, leveraged expression of two variables, AI demand and the cost of AI credit. When credit default swap costs on the AI cloud trade rose on July 29, the whole group sold off regardless of company specific news. When a demand signal arrived on July 30, it rallied more than 20 percent in a session. A 33 point swing in two days on the same fundamentals tells you the equity is trading on macro sentiment and financing spreads far more than on any single quarter's operations.

The bullish interpretation is that the rebound was larger than the drop and was anchored to real events, the Leidos deal and the AWS reacceleration, not just a technical bounce. The bearish interpretation is that a business whose stock can fall 9 percent purely because credit spreads widened is a business the market treats as a credit instrument first and a cloud second, which is precisely the vulnerability of the debt funded model.

CoreWeave and Leidos partner to build classified, sovereign AI cloud for US defense and intelligence agencies, and the stock pops

On July 30 Leidos and CoreWeave announced a collaboration to deliver secure, sovereign AI cloud services for the US Intelligence Community and the Department of Defense. Per the companies, services would run inside Sensitive Compartmented Information Facility accredited data centers, with CoreWeave providing its AI native cloud platform, infrastructure, networking, storage and orchestration for training and inference, while Leidos leads mission integration, secure architecture and customer delivery. Envisioned uses include intelligence analyst augmentation, cyber simulation and testing, synthetic data generation, digital twins and edge to cloud orchestration in classified environments. CoreWeave shares rose more than 16 percent on the news on July 30, part of the broader neocloud snap back in story two.

Read analysis ▾

Strategically this is the most encouraging CoreWeave specific item of the week because it attacks the company's biggest structural weakness, customer concentration, from a new direction. Federal and intelligence workloads are sticky, multi year, relatively price insensitive and hard for competitors to enter because of the accreditation barrier. A SCIF accredited AI cloud is exactly the kind of moat that is difficult to build and difficult to dislodge once won, and it diversifies revenue away from the handful of mega labs that dominate CoreWeave's book today.

The counter is that CoreWeave sits at the infrastructure layer here while Leidos owns mission integration and customer delivery, which means Leidos holds the government relationship and captures the systems integration value. Federal accreditation and procurement are also slow, so revenue will lag the announcement by quarters, and the government AI cloud space is contested, with Oracle, Microsoft and Amazon all holding entrenched authorized to operate positions. This is a strong strategic beachhead, not an overnight revenue event.

Skeptic's note: the partnership scope is confirmed via both companies' own materials, but no contract value, committed capacity or specific agency awards were disclosed. The framing about classified use cases is the companies' stated intent, not a list of signed programs. The 16 percent move is a single session reaction. Treat the partnership as real and confirmed and the revenue impact as unquantified and back loaded.

Why It Matters for CoreWeave

Directly and mostly bullish. This opens a defense and intelligence vertical that is high value, sticky and defended by accreditation barriers, and it is precisely the diversification the market has wanted given the concentration risk that drove the recent sell off. Sovereign and classified AI is also a category hyperscalers move through slowly, so a specialist plus a trusted integrator like Leidos has a real opening. The threats are that CoreWeave provides the commodity infrastructure layer while Leidos owns the customer, that federal revenue is slow to convert, and that Oracle, Microsoft and Amazon already hold government cloud authorizations CoreWeave will have to displace. Net, this is a genuine strategic positive whose financial payoff is real but deferred.

The cycle's circular financing shows up on the income statement: Amazon and Microsoft both book large paper gains on their Anthropic stakes

This edition's most analytically important thread is quieter than a data center lease. In the same earnings wave, both Amazon and Microsoft reported sizable non operating gains from their equity stakes in Anthropic. Amazon's roughly 53.4 billion dollar other income line, cited as primarily from Anthropic, was the single biggest reason reported net income near 62.6 billion dollars looked so large. Microsoft separately reported a gain of about 3.2 billion dollars from its Anthropic investment, described as adding roughly 33 cents to diluted earnings per share, connected to a 2025 investment paired with an Anthropic commitment to buy Azure capacity. The pattern is the circular financing of this cycle, chipmakers and hyperscalers funding the AI labs that in turn buy their compute, now flowing through the profit line rather than just press releases.

Read analysis ▾

For most of this cycle the circular financing story lived in announcements: Nvidia takes equity in a cloud that buys its chips, a hyperscaler invests in a lab that commits to its cloud. This quarter that same web materialized as reported profit. When a large slice of Amazon's headline net income is a mark to market gain on a private lab stake, the AI investment loop is no longer just a funding mechanism, it is a driver of earnings optics. That is worth watching precisely because paper gains reverse. The same marks that inflated this quarter's profit would deflate a future one if private AI valuations wobble.

The bullish read is that soaring Anthropic marks reflect a genuinely more valuable lab and a healthier ecosystem, and more capital flowing into AI labs ultimately means more compute demand downstream. The skeptical read is about earnings quality and reflexivity: the buyers of compute are being financed by the sellers of compute, and the resulting gains are now propping up the very earnings the market is using to justify continued capex. That is a loop that looks great while valuations rise and unwinds quickly if they fall.

Skeptic's note: the roughly 53.4 billion dollar Amazon figure and the about 3.2 billion dollar Microsoft figure are as reported in earnings coverage and should be confirmed against each filing, and the specific attribution to Anthropic versus other investments may be partial. These are non cash, mark to market gains on private holdings, not realized proceeds. Treat the pattern as the confirmed signal and each exact figure as provisional.

Why It Matters for CoreWeave

This is the corporate development lens on the week. Bullish: rising Anthropic marks mean CoreWeave's key anchor customers and the labs it finances capacity for are being valued ever higher, and capital keeps pouring into the ecosystem CoreWeave serves. Threat: the market is currently justifying record AI capex partly on earnings that are being flattered by paper gains inside the same circular loop, and if investors start discounting investment gains and demanding cash returns, sentiment turns against exactly the debt funded, capex heavy model CoreWeave embodies, raising its cost of capital. CoreWeave benefits from the loop while it spins up and is exposed if it spins down.

DeepSeek ships V4-Flash-0731, a cheap 284 billion parameter model retuned for agents that reportedly beats its own larger preview on agentic benchmarks

On July 31 DeepSeek released the official V4-Flash-0731 build, a re post trained version of its roughly 284 billion parameter model aimed at agentic and coding workloads. Per DeepSeek's own materials and third party write ups, the refreshed Flash outscored the larger V4-Pro preview across a set of agent benchmarks, with reported figures including Terminal Bench 2.1 around 82.7 and a large jump on a DeepSWE coding measure, while keeping pricing cited near 0.14 dollars per million input tokens. Coverage stressed that the upgrade is a silent, drop in replacement on the same API endpoint for existing users.

Read analysis ▾

The interesting part is not the leaderboard, it is the price and the shape. A cheaper, mid sized model tuned so heavily for agents that it beats a larger sibling on agentic tasks points at where inference demand is going: long running, tool using agent sessions where cost per token matters enormously because the token count per task is huge. Driving capable agentic performance down to roughly 0.14 dollars per million input tokens is the kind of price move that expands the total volume of agent workloads, which is compute demand.

The competitive and strategic subtext is China and open weight pressure. Ultra cheap, capable Chinese models compress global inference pricing and pull some workloads toward providers and geographies outside the US neocloud fleets. That is good for total AI compute consumption worldwide and more ambiguous for any single US merchant cloud's pricing power. The efficiency trend cuts both ways: cheaper intelligence means more usage, but also thinner margins per token served.

Skeptic's note: the benchmark figures are from DeepSeek's own release and aligned third party posts, not independently reproduced here, and vendor reported agent scores reliably flatter the vendor. The parameter count and pricing are as reported. Treat the direction, cheaper agent grade models expanding inference volume, as the durable signal and the specific scores as provisional.

Why It Matters for CoreWeave

Two forces pull in opposite directions. Bullish: cheaper, capable agent models expand the total volume of inference, and agentic workloads are the most compute intensive per user, which is the workload that fills rentable GPU fleets. Threat: relentless efficiency and ultra low pricing, led here by a Chinese lab, compress the price of inference and can thin the margins merchant clouds earn per token, and DeepSeek's own workloads largely run outside US neocloud capacity. The read to hold is that the agentic compute pie keeps growing, but price per unit keeps falling, so volume has to outrun compression.
