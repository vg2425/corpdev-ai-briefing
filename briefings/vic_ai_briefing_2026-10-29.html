Microsoft blamed its capex shortfall on delayed third party GPU capacity, pushed some of it into the first half of 2027 and trimmed its 2026 capex view, and CoreWeave, Nebius and IREN sold off even as Azure grew 48 percent

Microsoft reported fiscal Q1 2027 results on October 28. Azure revenue, disclosed in dollars for the first time under the narrower September definition, was 33.2 billion dollars, up 48 percent in constant currency against Microsoft's own guide of 44 to 45 percent. Total revenue was 92.3 billion dollars, up 19 percent. Capex including finance leases was 45.2 billion dollars against an outlook of more than 50 billion dollars and a reported analyst average of about 50.7 billion dollars. CFO Amy Hood said, per CNBC, that "some third party capacity deliveries we expected in Q1 were delayed" and that delays at third party GPU providers will push some capacity into the first half of 2027. CNBC reports calendar 2026 capex guidance moved to about 170 billion dollars from about 175 billion dollars, with more than 50 billion dollars expected in the December quarter. Hood did not name the providers. In overnight and premarket trading CoreWeave fell roughly 5 to 6 percent, Nebius about 6 percent, IREN about 5 percent and Applied Digital about 4 percent, while Microsoft rose about 4 percent after hours. Flag: the 170 billion dollar figure and the Hood quotes are from CNBC and other press accounts of the call; no full transcript was read. Flag: reported drops for CoreWeave range from just under 5 percent to 6.1 percent depending on outlet and time stamp.

Read analysis ▾

The market read is simple: Microsoft spent less than expected and said outside suppliers were late, and Microsoft is the largest disclosed neocloud customer. The more useful read is in the mechanics. Microsoft's capex line now excludes a growing share of leases because it extended data center building lives to 25 years in July, which pushes more new leases into operating lease treatment. Its 10-Q shows 6.5 billion dollars of new operating lease assets in the quarter against 1.4 billion dollars a year earlier, and 364.6 billion dollars of leases signed but not yet commenced, up from 329.1 billion dollars at the end of June. That is a large contracted pipeline for third party capacity, and it does not show up in the headline capex number that traders are reacting to.

Demand is not the issue. Hood has said demand exceeds supply, Azure beat its own guide for at least the fifth straight quarter by StockTitan's count, and the December quarter Azure guide is reported at 49 to 50 percent. Commercial RPO was 677 billion dollars, up 73 percent year on year but roughly flat sequentially, which Microsoft has warned would be volatile because of large OpenAI contracts signed last year. The constraint Hood described is delivery: GPUs, power and buildings arriving later than planned at partners.

Skeptic's note: confirmed from Microsoft materials as summarised by StockTitan are Azure revenue and growth, total revenue, the 45.2 billion dollar capex figure, RPO, the lease reclassification and the uncommenced lease balance. Reported from the call through CNBC and others, not checked against a transcript: the delay language, the first half 2027 timing, the cut to about 170 billion dollars and the December quarter capex figure. TipRanks framed the shift as into the second half of Microsoft's fiscal year, which is the same calendar window but worded differently. Nothing ties the delay to CoreWeave specifically; the selloff is guilt by association.

Why It Matters for CoreWeave

Threat read: this lands on the one thing the credit market already doubts, execution on delivery. If CoreWeave is among the late providers, revenue recognition slips while interest and depreciation do not, and a customer with Microsoft's leverage will use delays to renegotiate price or timing. Even if CoreWeave is not the laggard, the narrative "neoclouds cannot deliver on time" raises its cost of capital the week before Q3 results. Bull read: Microsoft is not cutting demand, it is waiting for supply, and 364.6 billion dollars of signed but uncommenced leases says the hyperscaler is still outsourcing at scale. Late capacity is still contracted capacity. Corporate development priority: be ready on November 10 to state plainly which Microsoft deliveries are on schedule, what slipped and why, because analysts will ask, and silence will be read as confirmation.

Google Cloud revenue doubled to 30.7 billion dollars with a 561 billion dollar backlog, and Alphabet said it began renting third party compute at the end of Q3 and will ramp it in Q4, but negative free cash flow sent the stock and the neoclouds lower

Alphabet's Q3 8-K shows revenue of 127.6 billion dollars, up 25 percent, and GAAP diluted EPS of 2.12 dollars, depressed by an 8.7 billion dollar equity securities loss that coverage attributes mainly to its SpaceX stake. Google Cloud revenue rose 103 percent to 30.7 billion dollars with operating income of about 11.1 billion dollars; cloud backlog reached 561 billion dollars, of which about 55 percent is expected to be recognised over 24 months. Search and other revenue grew 12 percent, down from 17 percent, and missed consensus by about 1.6 billion dollars, which Alphabet tied to AI Mode. Capex was 51.4 billion dollars against a reported 55.1 billion dollar consensus; 2026 guidance was held at 195 to 205 billion dollars, with a "significant" increase signalled for 2027. Free cash flow was about negative 9.1 billion dollars, a second negative quarter. Per call transcripts, Alphabet started using third party compute capacity at the very end of Q3 to relieve supply constraints and will ramp it in Q4, with near term margin pressure. Shares fell about 10 percent after hours; CoreWeave was down about 6 percent and Nebius about 6.5 percent premarket. Flag: the third party compute language comes from transcript summaries, not a full transcript read, and no provider was named. Flag: after hours declines are reported between 7 and 10.4 percent.

Read analysis ▾

Two things that matter to CoreWeave pull in opposite directions. Operationally, the biggest TPU owner in the world just said it cannot build fast enough and is buying outside capacity, a step it previewed in July and has now begun. A 561 billion dollar backlog growing faster than internal build is exactly the gap merchant GPU clouds exist to fill. Financially, Alphabet has now posted two quarters of negative free cash flow, and the market punished it for that more than it rewarded 103 percent cloud growth. Investors are signalling that capex funded growth is being priced on cash, not bookings, which is the same lens being applied to CoreWeave.

Combined with Microsoft, the overnight message to neocloud equity was "hyperscalers are spending less than expected this quarter," which is literally true and strategically misleading. Both companies undershot capex estimates on timing, while both reaffirmed or signalled higher multi year spend and both cited supply constraints. The neocloud selloff is a reaction to the near term print, not to a change in the demand curve.

Skeptic's note: revenue, EPS and the equity loss are confirmed against the 8-K as cited by FinanceFeeds. Cloud backlog, the 55 percent recognition window, capex, free cash flow and 2027 language are from Alphabet's release and call as relayed by several outlets. The SpaceX attribution of the equity loss is from coverage, not verified in the filing. Whether any of the third party capacity is GPU based, as opposed to TPU hosting by partners, was not disclosed.

Why It Matters for CoreWeave

Bull read: Google is now a confirmed buyer of outside compute, and CoreWeave was reported in 2025 to be in discussions to supply Google capacity (Flag: not reconfirmed in this run), so a Q4 ramp is a potential volume tailwind. A cloud business growing over 100 percent with a 24 month recognition window on half of a 561 billion dollar backlog will need bridge capacity for several quarters. Threat read: Google is also selling TPUs to third parties and onto OCI from 2027, so its bridge buying may be temporary and may favour partners hosting Google silicon. And the market's focus on free cash flow is a direct read across to CoreWeave's own negative free cash flow. Corporate development priority: determine whether CoreWeave is in Google's Q4 ramp and on what tenor, since a short bridge contract and a multi year take or pay are very different credit stories.

Oracle is CoreWeave's closest analogue in the credit market: a heavy AI capex builder whose bonds have traded as a proxy for AI infrastructure risk. The rest has to come from operating cash flow, customer prepayments, partner or vendor financing and off balance sheet structures, which is precisely where the cap's definitions are silent.

Skeptic's note: the cap, revenue target, free cash flow timing and leverage target are confirmed in Oracle's release and 8-K. Oracle self constraining also limits how fast it can absorb incremental AI demand, which can leave share for others. Threat read: Oracle is the most aggressive competitor for the same large lab contracts, now with Vera Rubin generally available and TPUs arriving in 2027, and a stronger balance sheet narrative makes it the safer counterparty in a lab's eyes. Corporate development priority: benchmark CoreWeave's Vera Rubin availability timing against OCI's announcement, since customers will.

The DOJ is asking whether NVIDIA's roughly 13 billion dollar Hugging Face purchase would give it control of a key AI model distribution point, folding the deal into its broader NVIDIA conduct probe

Reuters reported on October 28, citing sources, that the Justice Department is inquiring how NVIDIA's purchase of Hugging Face, valued at about 12.9 billion dollars and expected to close in the first half of 2027, would affect AI competition. The questions reportedly sit inside a years long investigation of NVIDIA's conduct covering acquisitions, investments and sales practices; Bloomberg reported last month that DOJ was also examining NVIDIA's roughly 20 billion dollar Groq licensing deal. NVIDIA declined to comment and DOJ declined to discuss specific matters. NVIDIA shares fell about 1.2 percent to 215.38 dollars. Flag: the inquiry is sourced to anonymous people; an inquiry is not a lawsuit or a finding. Flag: the deal's announcement month is given as September by most outlets and August by Benzinga.

Read analysis ▾

NVIDIA has spent the past year moving up and down the stack: SchedMD for cluster scheduling, Run:ai for orchestration, Groq for inference silicon, Hugging Face for model distribution, plus capacity prepayments and investments in its own customers. Each step makes NVIDIA more of a platform and less of a component supplier. The DOJ's reported interest is in the same thing CoreWeave's customers should care about, namely whether the company that allocates scarce GPUs also controls the software and distribution layers that determine which clouds models land on first.

Skeptic's note: confirmed are that Reuters published the report and that NVIDIA and DOJ declined comment. Everything about DOJ's questions comes from unnamed sources. US antitrust posture under the current administration has been permissive on AI deals, and DOJ's own statement emphasised prioritising "Super Intelligence" advancement, so a block is far from the base case.

Why It Matters for CoreWeave

Bull read: antitrust attention on NVIDIA's vertical reach makes it harder for NVIDIA to favour its own or a few preferred clouds through software and distribution, which protects independent GPU clouds' access. NVIDIA is also a CoreWeave investor and partner, and regulatory pressure may make it more careful to treat merchant clouds even handedly. Threat read: if scrutiny chills NVIDIA's willingness to backstop neoclouds through investments, capacity guarantees or utilisation floors, one of CoreWeave's support channels in a tight credit market weakens. Corporate development priority: track whether the conduct probe extends to NVIDIA's investments in and commitments to its cloud customers, since that is where CoreWeave would be directly exposed.

The compute relevant signal is in the deployment pattern rather than the scores. Google is shipping frontier models every two weeks, gating the strongest to a vetted audience, and marketing agents that run for days. Long horizon agents multiply inference tokens per task by orders of magnitude, and gated rollouts mean the heavy usage is still ahead of us. OpenAI is going the other way on price, cutting Terra's API cost in half, which also expands usage. Both point to rising inference demand, which is the workload most likely to be outsourced to merchant clouds.

Threat read: Google trains and serves Gemini on its own TPUs, so Gemini's success adds demand to Google's stack, not CoreWeave's, and it raises the bar for the labs that are CoreWeave's customers, compressing their pricing power and therefore their ability to pay. Corporate development priority: watch whether OpenAI's Terra price cut is matched by Anthropic and others, since a price war at the model layer eventually reaches compute contract renewals.

⚡ Medium Signal Power & Policy 6+ sources
NERC's board adopted the first five reliability standards for "computational loads," requiring planners to study data center impacts and operators to disclose how their facilities respond to grid disturbances

NERC's Board of Trustees adopted the standards in a special meeting on October 26, after a final ballot on October 14, as phase one of its large loads action plan. The standards direct transmission planners to study the reliability impact of new computational loads and require data center operators to disclose facility responses to grid disturbances. NERC will file them with FERC, which had directed a filing by December 31, 2026; they are not enforceable in the US until FERC approves them and phased compliance begins. Further standards on load performance, event reporting and model quality are in development. Flag: compliance dates and penalties were not found in the coverage reviewed.

Read analysis ▾

This is the reliability counterpart to the FERC PJM ruling covered in the prior edition. FERC sent the "who pays" question back to states; NERC is now defining "how a data center must behave on the grid." AI training clusters can swing power draw sharply and drop off the grid in a disturbance, which grid operators treat as a reliability risk. Disclosure today usually becomes ride through and performance requirements tomorrow, which means equipment, controls and potentially onsite storage.

Skeptic's note: the adoption, dates and content are confirmed by the American Public Power Association, RTO Insider and Daily Energy Insider. The obligations are not yet binding, and the next phase of standards, which will matter more for capital cost, is still in development.

Why It Matters for CoreWeave

Threat read: as an operator in many leased sites, CoreWeave will need disturbance response data from landlords and its own systems, and future performance standards could add retrofit costs or slow energisation at new campuses. Bull read: operators that can demonstrate grid friendly behaviour, through flexible load or battery buffering, may get faster interconnection, and a common standard reduces utility by utility uncertainty. Corporate development priority: add NERC compliance readiness to site and lease diligence, especially for facilities energising after 2027.
