Anthropic is in early talks to lease up to 10 billion dollars of compute from Meta, weeks after a 45 billion dollar SpaceX deal

Multiple outlets reported on July 17 that Anthropic is in preliminary talks to rent AI compute from Meta in a deal the New York Times pegged at as much as 10 billion dollars over two years. It lands weeks after Anthropic's reported 45 billion dollar, three year agreement to use SpaceX's Colossus 1 data center, and it doubles as Meta's first real move to sell its infrastructure as a cloud business.

Read analysis ▾

The reported structure is a two year arrangement with monthly payments, proposed by Anthropic in June, with either side able to exit early. Coverage frames it against Meta's own buildout: Zuckerberg said in May the company was weighing entering cloud computing to show investors a revenue path for its spending, and Meta has guided to roughly 125 to 145 billion dollars of capex this year, more than double last year. Renting spare capacity to a frontier lab is how a hyperscaler turns idle GPUs into cash.

The more important pattern is on Anthropic's side. In a matter of weeks it has reportedly lined up compute from SpaceX and now Meta, on top of its existing footprint across AWS Trainium, Google TPUs and Nvidia GPUs. This is a lab deliberately building a diversified, multi supplier compute portfolio rather than betting on a single provider, and it treats compute access as an existential procurement problem to be solved with balance sheet and contracts, not just cleverer models.

Skeptic's note: these are early stage talks and the 10 billion dollar figure is a reported ceiling from the New York Times, not a signed number, and either party can walk. Even the SpaceX Colossus terms are reported, not confirmed by filings I read. Read the direction as solid and the specific dollars as provisional.

Why It Matters for CoreWeave

This is the clearest window yet into how frontier labs actually source compute, and it cuts both ways for CoreWeave. The bullish read is that it validates the entire merchant compute leasing thesis: Anthropic is signing multi billion dollar, multi year capacity contracts because compute is its binding constraint, which is exactly the demand CoreWeave monetizes. The threat is who is now selling that capacity. When Meta and SpaceX rent out captive infrastructure, CoreWeave competes for the same lab customers against hyperscalers turned landlords with deeper balance sheets, and the labs' explicit multi vendor strategy is designed to keep pricing power and avoid lock in with any one supplier, CoreWeave included.

TSMC commits another 100 billion dollars to Arizona, lifting its US pledge to 265 billion and raising 2026 capex toward 64 billion

At its July 16 second quarter earnings call, TSMC disclosed an additional 100 billion dollar Arizona investment for at least four more advanced fabs and packaging facilities, bringing its total US commitment to a reported 265 billion dollars. It also raised 2026 capex guidance to roughly 60 to 64 billion dollars and lifted full year revenue growth guidance to slightly above 40 percent, with CEO C.C. Wei saying AI demand will stay robust through 2030.

Read analysis ▾

This extends the record quarter covered in the last edition into a concrete, multi year capacity commitment. The additional 100 billion dollars stacks on the previously announced 165 billion to reach 265 billion across a reported ten fabs and two packaging plants, and the capex raise pushes TSMC's annual spend past the ten trillion yen mark for the first time. The messaging is deliberate: the world's most important foundry is telling the market that AI compute demand is not just strong today but underwritten through the end of the decade.

The strategic weight is the US 2nm and advanced packaging footprint. Packaging, specifically CoWoS, has been the real bottleneck on GPU supply, so building more packaging capacity on US soil addresses the exact chokepoint that has throttled deliveries, while also derisking the supply chain geographically at a moment when Taiwan concentration is a live concern for large buyers and governments.

Skeptic's note: a 265 billion dollar figure and a capex raise are commitments and guidance, not spent dollars, and megafab announcements have a history of slipping timelines and inflating headline totals for political effect. Near record utilization and high margins also mean less room for upside surprise. Treat this as a strong demand signal and a statement of intent, not a delivered buildout.

Why It Matters for CoreWeave

Upstream foundry capacity is the hard ceiling on how many GPUs CoreWeave can ever rack, so a 100 billion dollar expansion plus a capex raise is TSMC underwriting the multi year demand thesis behind CoreWeave's backlog with its own balance sheet. More US based 2nm and packaging capacity is the single most direct lever on GPU availability and delivery timelines, and it derisks the geographic concentration that worries CoreWeave's customers and lenders. The catch is pricing: near record utilization and high margins mean the foundry keeps pricing power, and that cost flows through Nvidia into the price CoreWeave pays per chip.

Moonshot's Kimi K3 ships as the largest open weight model yet and takes the number one spot on the Frontend Code Arena, beating Claude Fable 5

On July 17 Moonshot AI released Kimi K3, a reported 2.8 trillion parameter mixture of experts model with a one million token context window, and it debuted at number one on the Frontend Code Arena, passing Claude Fable 5. The API is live now and Moonshot has dated the full open weights for July 27, making it the strongest open weight model shipped to date.

Read analysis ▾

The coding result is the headline: K3 reportedly led six of seven frontend domains on the Arena, losing only in Gaming to Fable 5, and coverage puts it level with Claude Opus 4.8 on some independent evals. Moonshot itself concedes K3 still sits behind Fable 5 and OpenAI's GPT 5.6 Sol on overall performance, but a Chinese open weight model beating a frontier closed model on any serious coding board is a real shift, and it arrives despite US compute export limits.

The part that matters most for infrastructure is the open weights date of July 27 and the pricing, reported at 3 dollars per million input tokens and 15 per million output. Open weights mean any enterprise can self host the model instead of calling a US lab API, which converts model quality directly into demand for rentable GPUs. This is the clearest recent example of the open weight flywheel: a capable model that anyone can run creates inference load that has to land on someone's hardware.

Skeptic's note: the 2.8 trillion parameter count, the Elo figures and the domain wins come from Moonshot's own materials and Arena boards, not independent audits, and a single leaderboard is a narrow slice of real world performance. Benchmark leadership is also fleeting in this cycle. The durable facts are that it shipped, the API is live, and open weights are promised for July 27.

Why It Matters for CoreWeave

A top tier open weight model is exactly the kind of release that pushes inference demand onto merchant GPU clouds. Once the weights drop on July 27, any enterprise that will not send data to a US lab API can self host K3, and self hosting a 2.8 trillion parameter model requires large, rentable GPU fleets, which is CoreWeave's core product. The counterweight is twofold: K3 is a Chinese model, so US enterprise and government adoption faces trust, security and compliance friction, and the aggressive token pricing compresses the economics of inference for everyone serving it. Net, open weight frontier class models expand the addressable inference pie even as they pressure margins.

It also reinforces the structural read that pushing frontier quality is getting harder, which drives labs toward more inference time compute and larger training runs rather than less, lifting the total compute pie regardless of which lab wins the next release.

Huawei showcases its Atlas 950 SuperPoD at WAIC, claiming 6.7 times the compute of Nvidia's next node, with Q4 availability

At the Shanghai World AI Conference around July 16 to 17, Huawei publicly demonstrated its Atlas 950 SuperPoD, a system linking 1,024 Ascend chips that it says delivers 1 EFLOPS FP8 and 2 EFLOPS FP4 with 256 TB of unified memory. Huawei claims it offers 6.7 times the compute and 15 times the memory of Nvidia's NVL144, with availability targeted for the fourth quarter of 2026 and a South Korea rollout planned.

Read analysis ▾

Huawei first previewed this system earlier in the year, but the WAIC floor demonstration and the concrete Q4 availability and export plans move it from roadmap to near term product. The architecture connects up to 8,192 NPUs in a full cluster, and Huawei separately claims its Ascend 950PR delivers roughly 2.87 times the inference performance of Nvidia's export limited H20 at about a quarter of the cost. Whatever the real numbers, the strategic message is that China has a domestically produced, cluster scale alternative to Nvidia that it is ready to sell abroad.

The setting matters. Showcasing this at WAIC, alongside Xi's keynote and the launch of a China led governance body, frames domestic silicon as a pillar of a parallel, non Western AI stack. The planned South Korea entry is the tell that Huawei intends to compete for customers outside China, not just serve the domestic market walled off by US export controls.

Skeptic's note: every headline performance figure here is Huawei's own claim, and vendor comparisons against a competitor's unreleased next node are marketing, not benchmarks. Yield, software maturity and real world utilization on Ascend remain the open questions, and "targeted for Q4" is not shipping. Take the direction seriously and the multiples with heavy skepticism.

Why It Matters for CoreWeave

This is the clearest sign yet that a credible non Nvidia, non Western compute stack is maturing at cluster scale, and it cuts two ways for CoreWeave. It reinforces the durable moat of Western aligned, export compliant, Nvidia based capacity for US and allied customers who cannot or will not touch Chinese silicon. But it also means the China market is effectively closing to the Nvidia ecosystem CoreWeave is built on, and Huawei's export ambitions could eventually contest third country customers on price. The near term effect is validation of CoreWeave's positioning; the longer term risk is a bifurcated global compute market where a large slice is simply unaddressable.
