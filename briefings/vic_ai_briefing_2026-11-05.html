🔥 High Signal Power & Policy Markets 10+ sources
Texas elected a governor who has promised a moratorium on all data center approvals, voters restricted data centers in at least 38 jurisdictions, and CoreWeave's 2031 bonds traded as low as 79.5 cents while Microsoft and Amazon rose

Democrat Gina Hinojosa beat Greg Abbott on November 3 and takes office January 19 with a Democratic Texas House. In an October 22 video she said "When I am governor I will issue a moratorium, a ban, on all data center approvals until laws can be passed by the Legislature," per the Texas Tribune. Her platform also calls for union labor requirements, water protections, an end to NDAs on projects and an end to the state sales tax break. This lands on top of Abbott's existing pause on TCEQ permits and ERCOT grid connections pending an audit, with ERCOT reports expected December 10. Nationally, Redbud Consulting counted 33 restrictive results among 45 local measures by the morning of November 4, and several Ohio cities voted to ban sites above 25 megawatts. Governors elect in Ohio (Amy Acton) and Georgia (Keisha Lance Bottoms) also campaigned on slowing construction, per Bloomberg. In credit, Bloomberg reported CoreWeave's unsecured 2031 notes traded as low as 79.5 cents on the dollar on November 4, with the 2030 and 2032 notes yielding above 15 percent. GuruFocus reports CRWV closed at 48.42 dollars, a 52 week low, after falling 8.6 percent on November 2 and rising 7.2 percent on November 3 per Trefis. Implicator reports Amazon rose about 7 percent and Microsoft about 5.5 percent in morning trading the same day. Flag: CoreWeave's November 4 move is reported as roughly 4 percent (CNBC, morning), 7.2 percent (Bloomberg, 10:24 a.m.), about 1.3 percent (Implicator, later quote) and nearly 10 percent at close (GuruFocus); confirm the close against an exchange print. Flag: the 38 jurisdiction and 33 of 45 tallies are from a consultancy count relayed by Implicator.

Read analysis ▾

The market priced this as a neocloud problem, and the reasoning is sound in direction even if the magnitude is not yet knowable. A permitting moratorium hurts whoever needs new approvals, new grid interconnections and new tax incentives to hit 2027 and 2028 delivery dates. Hyperscalers with existing campuses, entitled land and balance sheets can redirect capacity to other states or densify existing sites. A debt funded builder whose financing is sized to specific megawatts on specific dates has less room. That is why the Bloomberg bond move matters more than the equity move: credit investors are repricing delivery risk on collateral, and 15 percent yields are distressed territory for a company that will need to keep refinancing.

Several counterweights keep this from being a clean bear case. Nomura, cited by Seeking Alpha and Simply Wall St, expects the bigger impact from 2028 because 2027 capacity is largely already under construction, and one report says projects already under construction would reportedly be exempt from a permit freeze. Hinojosa's own powers are limited: Daybreak Wire's analysis says a governor can pause state permits by order, but the tax break, local veto rights and grid cost allocation need the Legislature, which convenes January 12. She also supported data center tax breaks in 2023, per Implicator. Outside Texas, the "backlash" read can overreach: Florida elected Byron Donalds, who backed development, and Iowa's governor elect opposed a moratorium. OpenAI's reported plan to rebrand its sites as "compute campuses" (Axios via TechRadar, confirmed as preferred term by an OpenAI spokesperson to Business Insider) shows the industry treating this as a public relations fight it expects to keep having.

Skeptic's note: confirmed are the election results, Hinojosa's on record moratorium pledge, Abbott's existing pause, and Bloomberg's bond prints. Not confirmed is any enacted statewide ban, its scope (approvals only, or interconnections too), whether projects in construction are exempt, and how many gigawatts are actually exposed. Estimates range from Wells Fargo's 27 percent of planned US capacity in Texas to GLJ Research's 13 gigawatts "immediately exposed," and Goldman economists' 45 percent national figure for short term moratoria is a scenario, not a forecast.

Why It Matters for CoreWeave

Threat read: CoreWeave leases capacity at Galaxy's Helios campus in West Texas and has other Texas exposure, so the market will now apply a political discount to every Texas megawatt in the backlog and to the collateral behind the 2030 to 2032 notes. Higher spreads compound the problem Nadella flagged last week: concentrated customers plus expensive debt plus delivery risk. Bull read: if new approvals freeze, already energized and permitted capacity becomes scarcer and more valuable, which favors operators with sites already built or under construction. The moratorium also pressures labs to buy from whoever can deliver now. Corporate development priority for November 10: disclose Texas megawatts by status (energized, under construction, permitted, pre permit), name any sites that are exempt under the existing TCEQ pause, and show the non Texas pipeline. Without that, investors will assume the worst case.

🔥 High Signal AI Infrastructure & Chips 5+ sources
Riot leased its entire 910 megawatt Corsicana, Texas campus to an unnamed "Frontier AI Lab" on a 15 year powered land lease worth about 28 billion dollars, with the tenant owning and operating its own compute

Per Riot's November 4 earnings release filed with the SEC, the lease is triple net, covers 910 megawatts of critical IT capacity, carries about 1.8 billion dollars of average annual rent including escalators, and rises to about 54 billion dollars if both five year extensions are exercised. Rent is payable on the full 910 megawatts regardless of utilization. Delivery is 400 megawatts in September 2027, 200 megawatts in January 2028 and 310 megawatts in July 2028. The structure is the notable part: Riot delivers land, power and powered pads; the tenant "will procure, install, own, and operate the modular compute deployment" and bears operating costs including power. Capital is about 6 million dollars per IT megawatt (about 5.5 billion dollars total), with Riot funding 2 million dollars per megawatt and reimbursing the tenant for the other 4 million in phases, funded with non recourse project debt or, failing that, as a rent credit. Riot says the tenant is the same lab that signed its Rockdale lease earlier this year. Riot's 10-Q dates the lease to October 28. Flag: the tenant is undisclosed; a podcast transcript previously linked Rockdale to Anthropic, which is unconfirmed. Flag: contract value, NOI margin and yield on cost are Riot's own figures.

Read analysis ▾

This is a template that bypasses the neocloud layer. A frontier lab is renting powered land directly from a former bitcoin miner and putting its own modular compute on it. There is no GPU cloud intermediary earning a spread on the hardware, no colocation operator building the shell, and the lab controls the stack. Riot's capital outlay is roughly half a Tier 3 build, which is why it can promise no new equity. Global Data Center Hub's Q3 review makes the same point at the industry level, describing labs becoming direct buyers of capacity.

It is also a Texas deal signed days before an election that may freeze new data center approvals. Riot's site is already energized for mining, which may help if a moratorium targets new interconnections, but the first phase does not deliver until September 2027 and the lab is carrying construction and political risk on its own compute. The tenant also agreed to rent on the full 910 megawatts regardless of utilization, a strong signal of demand conviction or of how scarce powered land has become.

Skeptic's note: confirmed are the signed lease, its term, phasing, capital split and Riot's reported totals in an SEC exhibit. Not confirmed are the tenant's identity, its credit support, whether project debt on "comparable market terms" will be available after this week's credit repricing, and whether a Hinojosa moratorium would touch phases two and three.

Why It Matters for CoreWeave

Threat read: the largest buyers are learning to self provision. If labs can rent powered pads and run their own modular racks, CoreWeave's role narrows to the parts of the stack labs do not want to own: fleet operations, software, financing and speed. Every powered land lease signed directly is capacity that did not go through a neocloud. Bull read: powered land is the scarce input, and leases like this validate the value of energized sites, which CoreWeave also controls through its own campuses and partners. Labs that self provision still need burst and short term capacity (see story 03), and operating a 900 megawatt fleet is hard. Corporate development angle: this is a structure CoreWeave could offer or acquire into, either as a powered shell landlord to labs or by partnering with miners that have energized Texas sites and no operating capability.

Anthropic's amended S-1 discloses about 895 billion dollars of compute and infrastructure commitments and a Q3 swing back to an operating loss, driven partly by 5 billion dollars of short term compute that "currently costs significantly more" than its long term deals

The S-1/A, filed November 2, adds third quarter results. Yahoo Finance reports Q3 revenue of about 17 billion dollars (up from 11.6 billion in Q2) and a 567 million dollar operating loss after a 974 million dollar Q2 operating profit. About 70 billion dollars of commitments come due within 12 months and about 447.5 billion before the end of 2030. Largest disclosed counterparties per Yahoo's chart: Broadcom equipment leases about 161.2 billion dollars, Google 121.3 billion, SpaceX's AI unit up to 120.5 billion, Amazon 110 billion, Microsoft 44.9 billion, Nscale up to 44.6 billion and Lambda 41.3 billion. A further 125 to 150 billion dollars of planned Google TPU capacity and more than 20 billion dollars with AMD have no fixed commitment and are excluded. Filing Insights reports that Nscale charges only for delivered capacity and that about 34.9 billion dollars of the Lambda contract ends if Lambda cannot secure its facility lease. Anthropic also estimates 5 to 7 percent of nine month revenue came from customers breaking its usage or commercial terms. The price range and share count remain blank. Flag: revenue and loss figures are from Yahoo Finance's read of the filing, not a direct read of the S-1/A in this run. Flag: Nscale and Lambda terms are from a Substack analysis of the filing.

Read analysis ▾

Two things in this filing matter directly to a GPU cloud. First, the demand signal: Anthropic says it books compute 12 to 18 months ahead, demand outran that plan, and it paid a premium for 5 billion dollars of short term capacity in a single quarter. That is the spot and short term market neoclouds sell into, and it shows a top lab paying up for speed. Second, the counterparty terms: the neocloud contracts that are visible are written to protect Anthropic. Nscale is paid only on delivery and Lambda's contract largely falls away if its lease falls through. That is the opposite of the take or pay certainty debt markets assume when they lend against neocloud backlogs.

The scale of the obligations, about seven times Anthropic's roughly 132 billion dollars of cash and short term investments per Yahoo, also makes the IPO a credit event for the whole supply chain. If the offering prices well, counterparties get a better funded customer. If it slips, every lender to a lab dependent builder will reprice. Roadshow timing remains press reporting; the earliest start under the 15 day rule is around November 10.

Skeptic's note: confirmed are the filing date, the 895.4 billion dollar headline, the near term and 2030 buckets, and that price range and share count are blank. Interpretive figures such as compute cost as a share of revenue and the "239x backlog" framing circulating on FourWeekMBA are third party calculations. CoreWeave does not appear among the largest named counterparties in Yahoo's chart; this run did not search the full filing for CoreWeave references.

Why It Matters for CoreWeave

Bull read: a frontier lab disclosed that it is short capacity and paying premium prices for near term compute, the exact product CoreWeave can deliver fastest from energized fleet. That is pricing power evidence ahead of November 10. Threat read: the disclosed neocloud contracts show labs pushing delivery and lease risk onto providers, and Anthropic's biggest commitments sit with Broadcom, Google, SpaceX and Amazon rather than with GPU clouds. If CoreWeave is pitching Anthropic, expect "pay on delivery" and lease contingent termination terms to be the starting point. Corporate development action: pull the full S-1/A and search for CoreWeave, then benchmark CoreWeave's own contract protections against the Nscale and Lambda terms before the call.

Microsoft declined an option to more than double its Texas contract with Nscale, worth "tens of billions" per the FT, after exiting Nscale's Loughton, UK deal over power delays

The Financial Times reported, and Reuters relayed on November 4 and 5 citing one source, that Microsoft will not exercise an option to expand its Texas agreement with Nscale. The source said the existing Texas contract is unaffected, the decision was made several months ago before Nscale filed for its IPO in September, and it has no impact on Nscale's total contract value of about 103 billion dollars disclosed in the IPO filing. Separately, Computing reported Microsoft terminated its agreement for Nscale's Loughton, Essex site after grid officials indicated power may not arrive until the 2030s. Nscale's 2025 announcements described the Texas site at about 240 megawatts with a Microsoft option for roughly 700 more from late 2027. Neither company commented on the record. Flag: the option value is the FT's characterisation; the 700 megawatt and 1.2 gigawatt figures circulating come from secondary outlets and were not confirmed against Nscale's filing.

Read analysis ▾

Read with Microsoft's October 28 comments, the pattern is consistent: Microsoft wants outside capacity now, is willing to walk from capacity that arrives late or on uncertain power, and does not want to grow optionality with a single neocloud supplier. The "decided months ago" framing is meant to reassure Nscale's IPO investors, but it also means Microsoft's appetite for incremental neocloud commitments was already cooling before its capex print. Last week's reporting that NVIDIA is discussing guarantees on Nscale construction debt for an Anthropic site now looks more like support for a supplier whose anchor customer is narrowing its exposure.

Skeptic's note: confirmed is that the FT and Reuters reported the decision from a source and that Microsoft exited Loughton. Not confirmed is the precise size of the forgone option, whether Microsoft has passed on similar options with other providers, and whether any of that capacity has been redirected to another neocloud.

Why It Matters for CoreWeave

Threat read: an expansion option is exactly the kind of contingent upside that sits in neocloud pipelines and investor models, and Microsoft just showed it will let those lapse. Analysts will ask CoreWeave whether any Microsoft options or extensions are outstanding and how many are at risk. Bull read: the UK exit was about power arriving in the 2030s, not demand. Providers with energized capacity in 2027 are still in a seller's market, and capacity Microsoft no longer wants from Nscale may be offered to labs that do. Corporate development priority: map which CoreWeave contracts carry customer options versus firm commitments and be ready to describe that split on November 10.

The headline is that a credible second source of rack scale accelerators is now shipping in volume to the same lab and hyperscaler customers NVIDIA serves. Those warrants are worth more as AMD's stock rises, so the GAAP drag grows with success. The inventory and receivables build ahead of a 17 billion dollar quarter is consistent with a real ramp, but it also concentrates execution risk in Q4.

⚡ Medium Signal LLM Model Releases Agentic AI & Tooling 10+ sources
Moonshot published weights for its 2.8 trillion parameter Kimi K3.1 Pro, OpenAI's agents produced working exploits for 948 Linux kernel flaws for about 1.2 million dollars, and OpenAI and Meta each bought a coding agent startup

Kimi K3.1 Pro weights landed on Hugging Face (moonshotai/Kimi-K3.1-Pro) on November 4 as promised at the October 23 API launch: about 2.8 trillion total and 104 billion active parameters, a 1 million token context, and a 1.56 terabyte download that reportedly needs about eight NVIDIA B300s to serve. The license reportedly requires a separate agreement for companies above 20 million dollars of revenue that host it as a service. Tencent launched Hy4, a 770 billion parameter model with computer use, through its products on November 4 per TechNode. OpenAI published an autonomous security pipeline that found 2,308 candidate Linux kernel flaws and built working local privilege escalation exploits for 948, at about 1.21 million dollars of API spend, and announced it will acquire Pierre Computer Company, whose code navigation tech already runs in Codex; Axios framed it as a step toward challenging GitHub. Meta acquired Conductor, which runs several coding agents in parallel; its cloud service shuts November 20. Flag: Hy4 was first released as a preview with open weights on August 28; the November item is a broader product launch, not a new model debut. Flag: Kimi parameter counts are vendor reported and the license summary was not checked against the license text; one source dates the upload to November 2. Flag: OpenAI's exploit counts are self reported.

Read analysis ▾

The compute angle in each item is direct. A near frontier open weight model that needs a full B300 node just to load is a hosting product, not a laptop download, and Moonshot's revenue threshold license pushes large hosts into a commercial deal. OpenAI's Linux run shows agentic workloads that are long, parallel and tool heavy, and priced at roughly 1,279 dollars per working exploit, which is the kind of sustained inference demand that keeps fleets busy. The two acquisitions show the coding agent layer consolidating into the labs and Meta, which means more agent traffic routed to first party infrastructure.

Counterpoint on demand: Apple reportedly cut some engineers' daily AI budgets from 300 to 200 dollars (Bloomberg via The Neuron), and Uber said only four rides were booked by AI agents in its busiest October week (TechCrunch). Enterprise cost discipline and thin consumer agent adoption are the bear case on how fast agent inference grows.

Skeptic's note: confirmed are the Kimi weights repository, OpenAI's research post and acquisition announcement, and TechCrunch's confirmation of the Conductor deal. Benchmarks, parameter counts and the B300 serving estimate are vendor or third party claims.

Why It Matters for CoreWeave

Bull read: Kimi K3.1 Pro is a ready made workload for GPU clouds. Enterprises that want a near frontier open model but cannot run a 1.56 terabyte, multi B300 deployment need a host, and CoreWeave can offer it with B300 capacity and a signed Moonshot hosting agreement if required. Long running security and coding agents are steady inference load. Threat read: Chinese open weights at this scale invite export and procurement scrutiny, so hosting them may carry policy risk with US government and sovereign customers. Coding agents consolidating into OpenAI and Meta pull that traffic toward first party data centers. Corporate development action: confirm the Kimi license terms for a hosted offering and assess whether US public sector customers would object.
