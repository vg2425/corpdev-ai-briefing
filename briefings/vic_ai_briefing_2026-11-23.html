Nvidia falls 14 percent to 125.53 dollars after Reuters reports an SEC enforcement inquiry into its accounting, a day after California's attorney general served an investigative subpoena; Bessent's 10 day clock on the board runs out around November 25

Reuters reported on November 20, citing two people briefed on the matter, that SEC enforcement staff are examining potential accounting issues at Nvidia. The SEC declined to comment and Reuters could not determine when the inquiry opened or exactly what it covers; coverage ties it to Nvidia's 2025 switch from billing location to customer headquarters in its geographic revenue table, which removed a Singapore line that had reached about 22 percent of revenue. The Wall Street Journal reported the probe also examines whether disclosures misled investors. On November 19, California Attorney General Rob Bonta served Nvidia with an investigative subpoena covering whether chips or confidential information reached China and whether the company misled investors, per AP, CBS and TechCrunch. Nvidia closed November 20 at 125.53 dollars, down 14.01 percent, per the Motley Fool's quote panel, and lost about 22.9 percent on the week per one market data site. Flag: the SEC inquiry is sourced to unnamed people; neither the SEC nor Nvidia has confirmed it.

Read analysis ▾

Thursday's edition treated the Huang exit as a governance and financing story. This week it became a regulatory one on three fronts at once: a federal securities inquiry, a state investigation that Bonta described to Politico as a first step, and Treasury Secretary Bessent's November 15 demand that the board explain within 10 days whether it ignored warning signs that chips sold to Singapore shell companies ended up in China. Nvidia also told its earnings call on November 17 that it had stopped shipping H200s to China despite holding some licenses, per TechCrunch, and Q4 guidance already assumes no China data center compute revenue. Nvidia has said its board hired Wachtell Lipton and former acting US Attorney Joon Kim for an internal review on November 10. The board still has no permanent CEO; the 8‑K sets no deadline, and reports differ on whether a search firm has been formally engaged.

Skeptic's note: confirmed facts are the subpoena (announced by Bonta's office), the share price move, and the 2025 change in the geographic revenue basis, which Nvidia disclosed. The SEC inquiry and its scope are reported only. An accounting professor quoted by Fortune said of the reporting change, "Nothing is illegal. It's disclosed." An inquiry is not a finding, and Nvidia settled its last SEC accounting matter in 2003 without admitting wrongdoing. The roughly November 25 deadline is derived from Bessent's November 15 remarks and is approximate.

Why It Matters for CoreWeave

Threat read: Nvidia is CoreWeave's sole GPU supplier, a major shareholder and the counterparty on capacity backstop agreements. A regulator questioning how Nvidia books and attributes revenue invites the same scrutiny of circular arrangements, including Nvidia's investments in and rent back agreements with its customers, and story 02's foundation pledge lands squarely in that frame. Distraction at the top of Nvidia, while it is "disciplined and selective" about neocloud support, raises the odds of slower decisions on allocation and guarantees. Bull read: none of this touches demand for Vera Rubin, and a supplier under regulatory pressure has every incentive to favour large, compliant, US headquartered customers with clean end users. Ask: an inventory of every commercial link to Nvidia (equity, backstops, rent back, foundation compute) with how each is disclosed, so the team can answer lender and auditor questions before they are asked.

🔥 High Signal Power & Policy AI Infrastructure & Chips 7 sources
Commerce's new interim final rule requires licenses for advanced chips to Indonesia, Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand and Brazil and for third country data centers serving Chinese companies; US headquartered firms are exempt with monthly reporting, and Moonshot reportedly cancels a 150 million dollar Indonesian B300 contract

Per NBC News, TNW and CNBC, BIS issued an interim final rule (text released around November 16, effective November 19 per reporting) with two parts. First, exporters need a license to ship covered advanced chips to Indonesia, Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand and Brazil, with US headquartered companies exempt if they file monthly reports. Second, data centers in third countries that serve companies headquartered in China, Macau or other D:5 countries above 12 million aggregate TPP, roughly 750 H100 equivalents, need a license; facilities already serving large Chinese customers have 90 days, putting the practical deadline in February. The Information, as relayed by several outlets, reports Moonshot AI cancelled a 150 million dollar contract to rent B300 capacity through an Indonesian data center. Trump and Xi met in Shenzhen on November 19 with no change to chip access; Bessent said the H200 remains the most advanced chip China can buy with licenses. Flag: the release date, exact TPP trigger wording and the US headquarters exemption should be checked against the Federal Register text; this item slightly predates the window but was not covered Thursday.

Read analysis ▾

The rule closes the remote access route that Southeast Asian neoclouds had built around Chinese demand, and it arrives as Bessent publicly blames Commerce for loose enforcement. CNBC reported on November 19 that Commerce had approved sales of more than 879,000 AI chips to China, which keeps congressional pressure high. Maybank expects delays and higher costs for Indonesian builds rather than an end to them. Accounts conflict on whether Moonshot has already signed a replacement contract with another regional provider or is still looking, including in Europe, where CNBC reported on November 5 that it had offered 30 to 50 percent premiums upfront.

Skeptic's note: confirmed facts are the existence of the rule and its broad structure as described by multiple outlets. The Moonshot cancellation is single sourced to The Information. Enforcement intensity, and whether "serving" means actual or potential access, differ across summaries.

Why It Matters for CoreWeave

Bull read: as a US headquartered operator, CoreWeave appears to qualify for the reporting exemption on shipments into Indonesia, while local and Chinese linked competitors for the same Jakarta capacity lose their largest demand source; that should improve its bargaining power with colocation landlords and push regional enterprise and sovereign demand toward compliant clouds. Threat read: CoreWeave announced in August three Indonesian sites totaling 360 megawatts for 2028, its only APAC footprint, and TipRanks flags it as the neocloud most exposed to the rule. Monthly reporting, KYC on end users and a softer Indonesian demand base add cost and slow the ramp. Ask: legal confirmation that the Indonesia program falls under the US headquarters exemption, the customer screening process for the 12 million TPP threshold, and whether any Indonesian pipeline assumed Chinese headquartered tenants.
