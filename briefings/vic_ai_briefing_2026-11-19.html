Nvidia beats and guides to 130 billion dollars, but Kress trims neocloud commitments to 32 billion dollars and promises to be "disciplined and selective"; the board rules out Huang's return and the stock gives back its gains

Nvidia's release of November 17 reports Q3 fiscal 2027 revenue of 115.8 billion dollars, up 103 percent year over year and 20 percent sequentially, with data center revenue of 107.6 billion dollars and a 74.8 percent GAAP gross margin. Q4 guidance, for a quarter with an extra week, is 130 billion dollars plus or minus 3 percent, assumes no China data center compute revenue and carries a 71 to 72 percent gross margin. Per Blockspace (via Yahoo Finance) and the CFO commentary it cites, Nvidia's contingent commitments to neocloud operators fell to 32 billion dollars from 36 billion dollars, while maximum exposure on data center guarantees rose to 118 billion dollars from 108.5 billion dollars, 105 billion dollars of which backs SB Energy's Ohio campus leased to OpenAI. Kress lifted the fiscal 2028 growth outlook to about 75 percent and cited a 2.5 trillion dollar cloud industry backlog. Chair Harvey Jones said on the call that the board does not intend to reinstate Huang, hours after Huang posted that he would be "honored" to return; Huang is reported to testify before Congress on December 1. Flag: Nvidia's November 18 move is reported as up 2.5 percent early (Yahoo), down 2.06 percent midday (Yahoo quote) and down 2.9 percent to 150.96 dollars at the close (Motley Fool ticker); treat the close as provisional.

Read analysis ▾

The headline numbers were never the risk; Monday's edition argued the quarter was contracted. The new information is in the commitments table. Blockspace reports the neocloud line, formerly labeled "AI cloud agreements," activates only if operators secure financing, deploy the capacity, make it operational and win Nvidia's acceptance, with nothing falling due in the rest of fiscal 2027 and a schedule of 6, 7, 6, 5 and 8 billion dollars across fiscal 2028 through 2032 and after. Supply commitments rose to 390 billion dollars from 279 billion dollars, mostly multiyear memory, and cloud service agreements plus uncommenced data center leases reached 102 billion dollars from 74 billion dollars. Asked by Bernstein's Stacy Rasgon whether guarantee conditions had changed, Kress said "Is it new? No. Did we change anything? No," and named land, power and shell as the priority for future support.

The call itself drew criticism. Yahoo's Brian Sozzi graded Kress "A" on numbers and "C" on optics, noting none of eight analysts mentioned Huang after Nvidia said it would not take questions on him. KeyBanc stayed Sector Weight on successor uncertainty; Barclays wrote that fiscal 2028 is now "underwritten." Nvidia also confirmed the 12.9 billion dollar Hugging Face acquisition announced in September remains pending, and formed a board committee on national security and export control.

Skeptic's note: confirmed facts are the reported results, guidance, the commitment and guarantee figures as disclosed, and Jones's statement. The 75 percent fiscal 2028 outlook and 2.5 trillion dollar backlog are management characterizations; the backlog is Nvidia's estimate for the cloud industry, not its own order book, and at least one live blog conflated the two. The December 1 testimony date comes from local syndicated coverage and was not confirmed against a committee notice. Flag: the 32 billion, 102 billion and 118 billion dollar figures measure different things and should not be summed.

Why It Matters for CoreWeave

Threat read: this is the first hard evidence that the "Bank of Nvidia" is tightening, not just a fear. A 4 billion dollar cut in neocloud commitments in one quarter, conditions that let Nvidia walk away if financing or delivery slips, and an explicit pivot of future support toward powered land rather than capacity backstops all reduce the implicit Nvidia put that lenders have priced into neocloud credit. Bull read: Nvidia's release names CoreWeave first among partners already running Vera Rubin NVL72 racks and among builders on Vera CPUs, which is a delivery credential that matters more when Nvidia is choosing whom to support selectively. A shift toward land, power and shell support also favours operators with a real pipeline of powered sites. Ask: how much of CoreWeave's 2027 and 2028 capacity plan assumes Nvidia backstop or rent back agreements inside the 32 billion dollar line, and whether any of those agreements carry the same financing and acceptance conditions.

Microsoft Ignite turns Azure into an agent control plane, with Foundry extended into AKS for open weight models, agents that can now create and change resources, and Cobalt 200 generally available; Nvidia pushes its own agent safety stack

In a November 17 post, Azure EVP Arun Ulag framed Ignite around "AI on infrastructure you control": Microsoft Foundry extended into Azure Kubernetes Service so customers can run models and agents with more control over performance and cost, workloads placed on available GPU capacity, and centralized security across agents, models and tools. Microsoft's infrastructure blog says the Azure Resource Manager MCP server now lets agents create, manage and update resources across more than 3,500 resource types, subject to Azure Policy. Azure Catalyst (agent led modernization), Azure HorizonDB and Cobalt 200 VMs are generally available, and Microsoft IQ was introduced as a context layer reachable from Claude and ChatGPT via MCP. Heise reports an Agent Grid for policy enforcement and VM isolated Agent Sandbox. Nvidia's Q3 release separately highlights its Open Agent Safety Platform and OpenShell reference architecture, which pairs Vera Rubin inference, Vera CPUs for agent execution and BlueField‑4 DPUs to monitor agent behaviour. Flag: preview versus general availability status differs across roundups; Microsoft's posts are the reference.

Read analysis ▾

The theme across both vendors is that agents need CPU heavy execution environments, sandboxes and policy enforcement alongside GPU inference. Microsoft is bundling all three into the hyperscaler control plane; Nvidia is publishing a reference design that any cloud can build. Ulag's point that "the same frontier models are available to everyone" and differentiation sits in customer context is a direct pitch against model only or GPU only providers.

Skeptic's note: these are product announcements; adoption claims (for example "nearly 60,000 customers" using Microsoft IQ) are company figures. None of this changes capacity this quarter.

Why It Matters for CoreWeave

Threat read: Foundry on AKS with managed GPUs is Microsoft offering enterprises the "run open weight models on capacity you control" proposition that neoclouds sell, wrapped in identity, governance and data that CoreWeave cannot match. Bull read: Nvidia's OpenShell design explicitly makes Vera CPUs and DPUs part of the agent stack, and CoreWeave is named as a Vera CPU builder; agent execution is a new, CPU and networking heavy workload CoreWeave can price, and Forge's sandbox tooling sits in the same slot. Ask: whether CoreWeave's Vera CPU deployments are being positioned for agent execution as a distinct SKU.

⚡ Medium Signal Power & Policy AI Infrastructure & Chips 6 sources
PJM asks FERC to halve large load study times to 12 months and require ride through for loads above 50 megawatts; in Texas, Giga Energy signs a 1 billion dollar, 10 year lease for 60 megawatts with an unnamed AI cloud provider

Per Utility Dive and PJM's own Inside Lines post, PJM and other grid operators filed large load interconnection proposals at FERC on November 16. PJM's filing would cut study time for new data center connections from 24 to 12 months by studying generation, transmission and large loads together, and would give states transparent access to data center driven transmission costs so they can bill large loads. A second PJM filing would require "Large Computational Loads" of 50 megawatts or more to ride through voltage and frequency disturbances, effective January 16, 2027 if approved. CAISO, ISO New England and New York transmission owners filed complementary proposals. Separately, Giga Energy announced on November 17 a 10 year, 1 billion dollar lease with an undisclosed US based AI cloud provider for Project Clay, a 60 megawatt modular campus in Hunt County, Texas, ready for service in spring 2027. Flag: the Giga tenant is not named; implied value works out to roughly 1.7 million dollars per megawatt per year, and the release does not say whether that includes power or services.

Read analysis ▾

The PJM filing follows FERC's rejection of most of PJM's earlier large load framework in October, per one secondary source. The Data Center Coalition has warned that missing an annual study cutoff could still mean nearly two years. On cost allocation, the direction of travel is clear: large loads pay more of the transmission bill. Giga's release is notable for courting Texas Governor elect Gina Hinojosa's priorities (self funded power, closed loop cooling, no draw on distribution serving homes), which signals how Texas developers expect permitting to work next year.

Skeptic's note: confirmed facts are the filings and the Giga announcement. FERC has not ruled; effective dates are proposed. The 12 month study time is a target, not a guarantee.

Why It Matters for CoreWeave

Bull read: faster PJM studies would shorten time to power for CoreWeave's mid Atlantic pipeline, and Nvidia's stated preference for supporting land, power and shell (story 01) rewards operators who already hold interconnection positions. Threat read: ride through mandates add capex (UPS, controls) to every 50 megawatt plus site and cost allocation reforms raise all in power cost; in Texas, small modular builds like Giga's give rival AI clouds a sub one year route to capacity. Ask: whether CoreWeave is the undisclosed Giga tenant, and the ride through compliance cost per megawatt across existing PJM sites.
